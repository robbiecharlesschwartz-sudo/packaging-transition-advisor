Our plan to increase recycling statewide

In 2025, the Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment approved Circular Action Alliance’s program plan, marking a major step toward free, convenient recycling for every Colorado household.

Under our oversight, this industry-funded and industry-run program shifts the cost of recycling from residents and local governments to the companies that produce and sell packaging. Additionally, it creates incentives for companies to design packaging that is more recyclable and reusable.

In 2022, Governor Polis signed the Producer Responsibility Program for Statewide Recycling Act into law, which directed CDPHE to establish a program requiring companies that sell packaging and paper products to fund a statewide recycling system for those materials. These companies formed an independent non-profit organization, called a Producer Responsibility Organization, to coordinate, fund, and manage this statewide recycling system. CDPHE appointed Circular Action Alliance as the designated Producer Responsibility Organization in May 2023.

Information for residents

Circular Action Alliance runs RecycleOn Colorado, the program’s public education website. Use it to:

  • Check what goes in your recycling bin.
  • Find recycling options where you live.
  • Sign up for updates on where free recycling is expanding.
    • Free recycling services are expanding in phases through 2030, so they may not be available in your area yet. RecycleOn Colorado is available in English, Spanish, and 10 other languages.

Latest updates and engagement opportunities

CDPHE’s Solid Waste and Materials Management Program is proposing changes to the Regulations Pertaining to Solid Waste Sites and Facilities to adjust the dollar threshold used to exempt producers from the program. Each year, the program is required to adjust for inflation and update the maximum amount of money a producer can earn and still be exempt from the Producer Responsibility Program. These adjustments are based on the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics' local consumer price index.

The program hosted a meeting with partners on the draft proposed rules on April 6, 2026. You can find recordings of rulemaking meetings and relevant materials in our Public Viewer Google Drive folder.

Many Coloradans — especially those in multi-unit housing or rural areas — currently lack access to recycling. The approved plan is projected to bring no-cost recycling to an estimated 700,000 more households.

A big jump in recycling rates: Colorado’s paper and packaging recycling rate is projected to increase from 25% to 58% by 2035 under the program.

Clear, consistent rules statewide: The program will standardize what can be recycled across Colorado and roll out educational tools to reduce confusion.

Financial relief for local governments: Municipalities will no longer pay to provide recycling. Circular Action Alliance will reimburse local governments for all net recycling costs.

Economic impact: Colorado is expected to see major economic benefits, including:

  • $31 million in additional annual wages.
  • Approximately 7,900 new direct and indirect green jobs.
  • Investments to lay the groundwork for a strong and growing circular economy in the state.

Environmental impact: By 2035, the program is projected to:

  • Avoid 1.3 million metric tons of greenhouse gas emissions annually — equal to taking 278,000 cars off the road each year.
  • Reduce landfill waste by 410,000 tons annually.
     

As of July 2025, a producer cannot sell or distribute products that use covered materials in Colorado unless they participate in the Producer Responsibility Program. 

Producers must pay producer responsibility dues on “covered materials” to the program in January 2026 and each year thereafter. Producers are also required to keep records and report data to document compliance with the law.

  • All producers of paper products and packaging materials should register with Circular Action Alliance and submit a supply report.  
  • Visit Circular Action Alliance’s producer resource center for guidance on registration and reporting requirements.

For questions and assistance, contact: producer.support@circularaction.org

  • All producers of applicable petroleum and automotive products under Interchange 360’s individual program should register with Interchange 360.

For questions and assistance, contact: memberservices@interchange360.com  

Depending on the covered materials a producer sells into Colorado, a producer may be required to register and report in both Circular Action Alliance and Interchange 360’s program.
 

Since the Governor signed the Producer Responsibility Program for Statewide Recycling Act into law in 2022, CDPHE, the Producer Responsibility Advisory Board, and Circular Action Alliance have been working to build this program from the ground up.
 

  • We began recruiting for the advisory board in late 2022, and the board held its first meeting in January 2023. Board members meet monthly to discuss and inform program implementation. 
  • A statewide needs assessment in 2023 mapped current recycling access and infrastructure across Colorado. The needs assessment included three projected scenarios to increase the state’s recycling and collection rate.
  • After a public comment period, we presented our recommendation to Colorado’s Joint Budget Committee in March 2024. The committee accepted our recommendation to adopt the medium scenario outlined in the needs assessment.
  • In 2024 and 2025, Circular Action Alliance gathered extensive feedback from various partners to shape the final program plan, which we approved in December 2025.
  • The plan approval puts the program on track for implementation and major improvements to recycling access and environmental outcomes statewide.
     

In December 2025, CDPHE’s executive director approved the Circular Action Alliance’s Program Plan, and the alliance must begin implementing this plan by the summer of 2026. The program plan describes how Circular Action Alliance will implement the program. It covers a range of topics, including how they will:

  • Assess the dues that producers must pay into the program. 
  • Manage recycling systems across the state for packaging and paper products. 
  • Set performance targets to increase recycling rates and post-consumer recycled content in products. 
  • Invest in new recycling infrastructure.
  • Promote the growth of responsible end markets for recycled materials.
  • Increase public awareness of recycling best practices.

CDPHE’s executive director approved Interchange 360's Individual Program Plan for automotive lubricant packaging in September 2025. As such, Interchange 360 kicked off its program in March 2026.
 
While Circular Action Alliance manages statewide recycling systems for standard consumer packaging and paper products, Interchange 360 manages the recycling of automotive lubricant packaging, such as motor oil and antifreeze. Since these containers contain residue unwanted in single-stream recycling, Interchange 360 collects these materials directly from commercial vendors and a state-wide network of automotive lubricant packaging drop-off sites. 

In November 2025, the Solid and Hazardous Waste Commission approved the final Section 18 regulations. These amended regulations included the addition of producer eco-modulation in Section 18.9, the addition of producer dues eco-modulation factors in Section 18.2.7, and various administrative updates. The eco-modulation amendments added to Section 18 create a framework for financial incentives that encourage producers to design and use packaging that is more recyclable and reusable.
